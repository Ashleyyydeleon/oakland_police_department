# Dispatcher analysis

Generated by [`dispatcher_analysis.py`](../../dispatcher_analysis.py). The same PNGs are written to Box at
`02 Oakland Police Dept (OPD)/Outputs/dispatch_*.png`.

A 911 call waits in two queues, each limited by a different group of staff:

```
Phone rings → answered → incident created → officer dispatched → en route → on scene
|__ Queue 1: call takers __|                |__ Queue 2: available officers __|__ travel __|
```

The CAD log starts at "incident created", so Queue 1 (the phone wait) is invisible in it.

## Findings (2025)
- P2/P3 calls wait a median of 1–8 hours for dispatch; travel is 5–11 minutes. The delay is waiting for a free officer.
- P1 waits spike at **6am and 2pm in every Area**, entirely in the dispatch queue, not travel. 2pm matches the 1400 patrol watch start. Areas 4 and 6 start at 0700 yet still spike at 6am, so the 6am spike may have a citywide cause. The City Auditor found dispatch-center minimum staffing is lowest from 5–8am.
- 911 answer speed was 67–79% within 15s each month (state standard 90%), with dispatchers in post at 66–78 vs. 90 recommended.

## Limitations / data to request
Answer-speed data only covers Jan 2025 – Jul 2026, so it can't yet be tested against staffing. Requests to OPD (or the
City Auditor, whose 2025 audit used them): hourly ECaTS (calls, answer time, abandoned calls), dispatch-center shift sheets
(actual vs minimum staffing), the dispatcher schedule by position, and the CAD unit-status log.
